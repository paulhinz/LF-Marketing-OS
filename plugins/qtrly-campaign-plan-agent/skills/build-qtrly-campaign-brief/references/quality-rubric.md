# Quality Rubric — score before delivery (all must pass)

## Traceability to the plan
1. Every campaign names at least one goal from the quarterly plan, and every plan goal with "increase" or "hold" direction has at least one campaign; deemphasized goals have either a maintenance campaign or an explicit "no campaign this quarter" note.
2. Campaign KPIs roll up to goal KPIs (coverage matrix present; shortfalls flagged, not hidden).
3. Campaign budgets under each goal sum to at most the goal budget; the discretionary reserve is left unallocated and labeled for HOT campaigns.
4. No goal was redefined. Goals the campaign set cannot reach are listed in Open Items for the ED/PL.

## Campaign completeness
5. Every campaign has the full Campaign Definition field set, including a named leader (or an explicit role placeholder flagged in Open Items), involved teams, RACI, KPI with source, budget, dates, thresholds, risks, dependencies.
6. Every campaign has a campaign type/stage from the matrix, and its sources match that stage (no cold paid ads for a Nurture campaign; no newsletter-only plan for General Awareness of contacts we do not have).
7. Every campaign names its mediums, and under each medium its specific sources, using the channel-map names.

## Source completeness (the hand-off test)
8. Every source table has all 13 rows from `brief-template.md` §4.n.4 filled: target market, ICP/persona, journey stage, key messages, assets with spec and due date, CTAs with destination/UTM/conversion event/hand-raiser flag/stage advanced, tactics and cadence, owner and tool, tracking, creation agent, execution agent, paid budget where relevant, notes/shared-resource flag.
9. A content creator could produce each asset from its row plus the foundation documents alone, without asking what the audience, message, or CTA is.
10. An execution lead could set up and schedule each source from its row plus §6 alone, without asking which list, tool, dates, or approvals apply.
11. Campaigns with more than one message have a Message Sequence table that groups all messages across sources in chronological order; per-source tables reference sequence numbers.

## Messaging integrity
12. Every key message maps to a Message Foundation pillar and proof point (or is labeled "inferred draft — confirm" when the Message Foundation is missing); at least one message per persona addresses a buying criterion.
13. Personas, ICPs, and segments come from the ICP document (or are labeled lower-confidence when it is missing); none are invented.
14. CTAs are stage-appropriate and hand-raiser CTAs are placed in Nurture-stage sources (moving Engaged contacts to Sales Ready), not on cold awareness assets.

## Agent routing
15. Every source names a creation agent and an execution agent by exact name, or "Agent TBD — manual: owner/tool"; the Agent Run Plan is ordered by dependency and lists the agents that do not yet exist.
16. Asset Production List and Execution Setup List are complete and consistent with the per-source tables (same assets, same owners, same dates).

## Data integrity and process
17. No invented figures: every number traces to LFX, a document (name, section), HubSpot, or "(manual — who)", recorded in §9.3.
18. Shared LF resources used by any campaign are flagged in §8.2 with a coordination owner.
19. Phases 1–3 were confirmed by the user (interactive mode), or, in draft mode, every unconfirmed choice is labeled DRAFT and listed in Open Items, with missing figures written as "(manual — needed)" rather than estimated.

## Document quality
20. Structure follows `brief-template.md`; sections numbered; Brand Kit styling applied when available; tables readable (landscape where needed); PDF render checked page by page.

## Scoring
Pass/fail per criterion. On any failure, revise and re-score. Maximum 3 revision cycles; then deliver the best draft with a plain list of still-failing criteria at the top of Open Items.
