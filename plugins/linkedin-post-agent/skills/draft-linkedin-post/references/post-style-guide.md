# LinkedIn Post Style Guide

Apply every rule below to every post drafted.

## Structure

- Start with a hook: a surprising stat, a bold claim, a question, or a scene-setting sentence. Never start with "I".
- Include a specific insight or perspective only the user could bring, not just a summary.
- End with either a question to spark comments, or a clear call to action.
- Write as if speaking to a smart colleague, not a press release audience.

## Language rules

- Avoid AI giveaway phrases like "dive into", "unleash", or "game-changing".
- Be direct and concise. Cut extra words.
- Maintain a natural tone. Write like people actually talk. It's fine to start with "and" or "but".
- Skip marketing language. No hype, no exaggeration.
- Simplify grammar. Casual grammar is okay if it feels more human.
- Cut the fluff. Skip extra adjectives or filler words.
- Do not use lists or sentence structures with "X and also Y".
- Do not use emdashes in writing.
- Avoid rhetorical questions like "Have you ever wondered...?"
- No fake engagement phrases like "Let's take a look", "Join me on this journey", or "Buckle up".
- Match the tone to feel human and authentic, not robotic or promotional.

## Content priorities (ranked)

1. Share a perspective or insight, not just the news
2. Connect the announcement to a larger trend or implication
3. Give credit to the community or contributors involved
4. Invite conversation

## Always include (when relevant)

- A link placeholder: [LINK]
- The Linux Foundation or relevant project name (spelled correctly), linked to their LinkedIn page if applicable
- A human angle: who worked on this, who benefits, why it matters

## Never include

- Phrases like "Thrilled to announce", "Excited to share", "Game-changer", "Paradigm shift"
- Excessive hashtags (3-5 max)
- Starting a post with "I" as the first word
- AI-generated filler phrases that add no information

## Format

- Write the post as plain text, ready to copy-paste into LinkedIn.
- Do not use markdown headers or bullet points inside the post itself (LinkedIn renders plain text).
- Use line breaks to create visual rhythm.
- Keep emojis to 0-2 per post, and only if natural for the user's tone.
- Length: 100-200 words is ideal; never more than 250.
