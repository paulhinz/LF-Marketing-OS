# Create content: blog post

Read the shared rules first: the content-core skill. Follow its step 1 (brief, guided or your own prompt, and cancel), step 2 (sources of truth, including references and, when it exists, curated content), 2a (companions), 3 (plan), 4 (voice and the clean asset), 5 (build and accessibility), 6 (self-review), 7 (the asset and its separate metadata record), 7a (revise, edit and delete) and 9 (hygiene). This skill adds only what is specific to a blog post.

## What is specific to a blog post

a headline that states the point, a TL;DR or short opening that gives the answer, scannable subheads, a proof point per section, a "so what" line where it helps, one call to action. Produce two alternative titles and a meta description (155 characters or fewer) as metadata fields, not in the post. Put a clearly marked placeholder where a code sample belongs rather than inventing code. Content the person pastes into the brief field, and any links or uploads, are sources (step 2, C and D), not text to paste into the post.

- Before choosing a topic, read the site's blog and news pages (core step 2, B). Do not draft a post that repeats an announcement already published; pick an angle that adds something, or say it already exists.
- Run SEO Writers' audit-tone-honesty, audit-paragraph-structure, audit-eeat, audit-useful-action and cold-reader-review on the draft, and Marketing's seo-audit if installed (core step 2a and 6). Use visual-storytelling if the post needs a diagram or chart; for an infographic, hand off to create-content-infographic.
- Format: the host's document type, or .docx or .md if a file is asked for by name.
- Metadata fields to fill for a post: titles, meta_description, byline (metadata only), placeholders, please_confirm, refresh_before_publishing.

## Copy-ready brief

```
Draft a blog post with the Marketing OS create-content-blog skill.

Project: [project name]
Topic or news hook: [what happened, or what we want to say]
Audience: [who reads it, e.g. platform engineers, members, press]
Goal: [what the reader should think or do afterwards]
Call to action and link: [e.g. join as a member, read the spec]
Length: [e.g. 600 words, 900 words]
Facts to include: [announcements, dates, names, numbers, with a source for each]
Quotes (optional): [exact wording, who said it, approved or not]
Anything to avoid: [topics, partner names, claims]
Sources and references (optional): [links, uploaded files, pasted text]

Use the project's brand and positioning outputs and its website content for tone.
Read the references for facts and tone only, and check them against the key numbers.
Give me a one-screen plan first, then the draft.
Keep the post clean: return status, byline, sources, references, titles, meta description and anything to confirm as a separate metadata record, not in the post.
Mark any number or claim you can't support as "please confirm" in that record.
```
