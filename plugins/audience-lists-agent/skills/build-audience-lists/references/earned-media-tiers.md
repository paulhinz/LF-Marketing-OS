# Earned media tiers

Source: LFX Marketing OS strategy deck ("2026 LFX Mktg OS Strategy", Google Slides `1PaQyPyExDxPevfUH6s4u3Dkf88_C9q9YfOwHN6WShtY`), slide 12, "Segmentation: Earned Media List", read October 6, 2026. Re-read the live slide when the Drive connector is available; the deck is the source of truth and this file is a snapshot.

Earned media serves four purposes: (1) legitimacy with members' executives and boards, (2) legitimacy with policymakers and regulators, (3) credibility with developers and maintainers, and (4) reaching the specific industry a foundation serves.

## The five tiers

### Tier 1: Business and finance (member-executive legitimacy)

Outlets: Reuters, Bloomberg, Wall Street Journal, Financial Times, Axios (especially Axios Pro Rata and Axios AI+), Forbes contributors, CNBC, Fortune, The Information.

These are hard to earn and need a news hook: a major member joining, a funding or donation figure, a governance decision, a CEO quote. They matter most for LF corporate, PyTorch Foundation, AAIF and Agentic AI, OpenSSF, and anything where the audience is the member's CFO or General Counsel.

### Tier 2: Policy and government

Outlets: Nextgov, FedScoop, Politico Pro (Tech and Cyber), Punchbowl, The Record, CyberScoop; Euractiv and Politico Europe for the EU.

OpenSSF, LF Europe, LF Energy, Open Mainframe, and anything touching the Cyber Resilience Act or AI regulation should treat these as primary, not secondary.

### Tier 3: Tech trade (developer and CTO credibility)

Outlets: TechCrunch, Wired, The Verge, Ars Technica, VentureBeat, SiliconANGLE and theCUBE, The New Stack, InfoWorld, SDxCentral, DevOps.com, ZDNet, The Register.

This is where CNCF, LF AI and Data, PyTorch, and OpenJS live. Most of the LF's realistic volume comes from here.

### Tier 4: Newsletters and independent voices

Outlets: a16z, From Inside AI, Stratechery, Import AI, Latent Space, Interconnects, Platformer, Last Week in AI, Pragmatic Engineer, Changelog, TLDR.

These are now higher-leverage than many Tier 3 outlets for AI, cheaper to earn, and they are what AI answer engines increasingly cite. Worth a dedicated list.

### Tier 5: Vertical industry press, per foundation

Examples from the deck: LF Energy: Utility Dive and Canary Media. FINOS: Waters Technology and American Banker. LF Decentralized Trust: CoinDesk. Automotive Grade Linux: Automotive News. LF Health and Agentic AI in healthcare: Fierce Healthcare and STAT.

These rarely overlap, so each foundation needs its own list. Build it from the ICP document's target markets and the personas' trusted sources. Further examples by market, to be checked against the personas before use: payments and fintech: PYMNTS, Payments Dive, American Banker, Finextra, The Block, CoinDesk, Fortune Crypto and Bloomberg Crypto desks, fintech newsletters (Fintech Brainfood, This Week in Fintech); telecom: Light Reading, Fierce Network; cloud and infrastructure: The New Stack also counts here; embedded and automotive: EE Times, Automotive News; public sector: GovTech.

## How to re-order the tiers per project

The tier numbers are a catalog, not a priority order. For each project:

1. Read the business outcomes in priority order from the ICP document (for example membership growth, developer adoption, ecosystem integrations).
2. Map each outcome to the tiers that reach the persona who owns it: membership growth → Tier 1 and the Tier 5 press the Premier lookalike set reads; developer adoption → Tier 3 and Tier 4; policy exposure → Tier 2; industry reach → Tier 5.
3. Order the tiers by the outcome they serve, highest first, and write one paragraph per tier explaining the placement. Label each group with its slide-12 tier number so the mapping back to the deck is visible.
4. Within a tier, list outlets from highest to lowest fit. Add outlets the personas' trusted sources name and outlets that already covered the project (news items in the social listening window). Drop outlets with no persona fit to a "release distribution only" row rather than deleting them.
5. Tier 1 is pitched only with a hook. Tier 2 is a watch list unless the project has a regulatory angle, in which case it becomes primary.

## Per-outlet row

Every outlet row carries four cells: the outlet; the fit (which persona reads it, and any prior coverage); the strategy (what to pitch, when, through whom, with which hook); and the lead message and proof (a pillar key message, value message, sound bite, or objection stance, with Stat Bank IDs). Rules from the Brand Kit apply verbatim to every message.
