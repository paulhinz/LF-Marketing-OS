# Workbook Template — slide-by-slide outline with the set questions

Deck name: `[Project] Hot Campaign Workbook — [Trigger short name] — DRAFT v1.pptx`
Length: 14 to 18 slides plus a 2 to 3 slide appendix. Same skeleton for every project and trigger.

Badges: `DRAFT — CONFIRM OR CORRECT` on every pre-filled slide. Question blocks are labeled `ED INPUT` or `CFT INPUT`, lettered, with an empty answer box under each question. When the runner is the ED, the ED INPUT blocks are pre-filled with their interview answers and badged `FROM INTERVIEW — CONFIRM`.

Slide notes carry provenance for every figure and claim. Colour and type come from the project's Brand Kit through `lf-output-formatter`; the hot-campaign badge colour is a warm accent so the deck is visibly different from the quarterly plan workbook.

---

## 1. Cover

`[Project]` · **Hot Campaign Workbook** · `[Trigger short name]` · Status: DRAFT v1 · Date · Prepared by (runner) · "Return by: [date, 24 hours after send]".

## 2. How to complete this workbook

Three short paragraphs:
- What this is: a proposed hot campaign, raised because [trigger type], to be decided in the next 24 hours. It adds to the quarterly plan; planned campaigns continue.
- What we need: the ED answers the ED INPUT boxes and marks the decision on slide 13; CFT leads answer the CFT INPUT boxes. 15 to 20 minutes. Correct any draft that is wrong rather than rewriting it.
- What happens next: messages approved starts the 48 to 72 hour clock; the completed workbook feeds `hot-campaign-plan-agent`, which produces the Segmentation List and the Hot Campaign Plan; assets go live within 72 hours after a QA gate; daily pulse; close-out report. Contact: [runner].

## 3. Hot Campaign Card (one page; the whole campaign)

Two-column card. Every row is drafted; every row has a source in the notes.

| Row | Content |
|---|---|
| Trigger | One sentence in plain words, with the date it happened |
| Trigger type / Archetype | From `trigger-taxonomy.md` |
| Why now | The window: decay date, and the live-by date (messages approved + 72h) |
| Business outcome / Goal served | Adoption, Fill the Seats, Grow the Audience, Secure/Retain Commitment, Education; the quarterly goal it rescues or "net new" |
| Primary intent | Create new contacts or nurture existing contacts toward [outcome] |
| Target (SOM) | One sentence: persona, stage, tier, estimated reachable count (est.) |
| Message angle | One sentence tied to a named pillar; the buying criterion it speaks to |
| Primary CTA | From the CTA library tier that matches the stage |
| Sources | The 3 to 6 owned sources proposed, by medium |
| Assets | Pull-forward / repurpose items from the Content Plan; net-new items to build |
| KPI and close | One primary KPI with target and baseline; close at [end date] or when KPI met |
| Budget ask | Amount from the discretionary reserve; reserve remaining after this ask |
| Owner / Approver | Who raised it; who approves messages |
| Decision requested | Go / No-Go / Park, by [date] |
| Missing inputs | Any core document, plan, report, or connector not found |

## 4. Opportunity Radar (scan mode only; in flag mode, retitle "Your trigger against the field")

Ranked table: rank, candidate, trigger type, archetype, composite score (6 to 30) with the six factor scores, decay date, one line of evidence with source. Selected candidate highlighted. Notes explain the rubric in two sentences.

`ED INPUT`
a. Is the highlighted candidate the one you would run? If not, which, and why?
b. Is anything missing from this list that you have heard from members, the board, or the community this week?

## 5. Section 1 — The Trigger

Left: what happened, when, who said it, primary source; what the newsletter said about it (issue date); what competitors or adjacent projects have already said. Right: social listening panel: 30-day volume with the spike marked, sentiment split, up to 5 voices with reach and one line each, the questions people are asking. Verification status shown as Verified / Unverified.

`ED INPUT`
a. Is this the moment you see? What did the data miss or get wrong?
b. What is the story only [Project] can tell here, in one sentence?
c. Who outside the marketing team should hear about this before we act (board, TOC, member, legal)?

## 6. Section 2 — Fit with the Quarterly Plan

Pace table: goal, KPI, target, quarter-to-date actual, pace (ahead / on pace / behind), campaigns serving it, sources in use. Below it: which goal this hot campaign serves (rescue) or that it is net new; overlap with running campaigns (shared list, shared source, same persona in the same week); the explicit list "these planned campaigns continue unchanged"; shared LF resources already booked this quarter. For rescue triggers, a line on what has been tried inside the existing campaign and why adding a tactic there is not enough.

`ED INPUT`
a. Rescue or net new? Which goal should this campaign's results count toward?
b. Is there anything in the plan you would pause or slow to make room? (Default: nothing.)
c. If this succeeds, what changes in next quarter's plan?

## 7. Section 3 — Target and Intent

SOM in one sentence (persona, stage, fit/warmth tier). Persona card(s) from the ICP: pains, motivation, blockers, preferred channels, the buying criterion in play. Estimated reachable count with source and definition (HubSpot segment, LFX, newsletter subscribers, community members) marked est. For new-contact intent: where the contacts come from. Hand-raiser actions that count as immediate wins. Exclusions (current members if the outcome is new membership; registered attendees if the outcome is registration).

`CFT INPUT`
a. Does a list or segment for this target exist today? Name and size.
b. What exclusions or suppressions must apply (recent sends, opt-outs, sales-owned accounts)?
c. Who owns the list and can have it ready within 24 hours?

## 8. Section 4 — Message and Stance

Message angle and the pillar it comes from. Table: persona × (key message, proof point from the Stat Bank with verification status, CTA). Stance box for news, competitor, and viral triggers: what we say, what we do not say, who approves. FAQ: 5 likely questions (from social listening and the objections section) with one-line draft answers. Voice reminders from the Brand Kit. Whether an ED-voice post is proposed.

`ED INPUT`
a. Your sound bite, in your own words.
b. Anything we must not say, name, or compare?
c. Do you want this in your voice (a post or quote under your name), the project's voice, or both?

`CFT INPUT`
d. Any proof point here you would not stand behind today? Replace it or strike it.

## 9. Section 5 — Assets and the Content Plan

Table from the Content Plan: asset, type, owner, scheduled date, status, and the proposed action (pull forward / repurpose / re-angle / no change). Then the net-new asset list required to go live: for each, type, source it serves, owner or creation agent, ready-by date within the 72 hours. Reuse before creating: note anything in Drive or the Content Hub that already fits.

`CFT INPUT`
a. Which of these can be ready within 72 hours of message approval? Which cannot, and what is the smallest version that can?
b. Who builds each net-new asset, and what would block them?
c. Is the Content Plan owner willing to move the marked items?

## 10. Section 6 — Lead Sources and Tactics

Table from the mediums-and-sources catalog (the same names the channel registry and dashboards use): medium, source, owned by (confirmed / unconfirmed), tactic and cadence for the campaign window, asset it uses, CTA, UTM (`utm_campaign=[campaign ID]`, `utm_medium`, `utm_source`; one UTM per source), execution tool and owner, shared-resource flag. Sources are matched to the intent and stage (Paid, Social, Web, earned PR for new contacts; Email, Newsletter, Direct Message, community channels, Retargeting for nurture). Paid rows show the budget slice.

`CFT INPUT`
a. Confirm we own and can publish to each source in the window. Strike any we cannot.
b. Which shared LF resources need booking (newsletter slot, banner, LF social, LF paid), and is there a conflict with another project this week?
c. Paid: can the audience, creative sizes, and landing page be live in 72 hours? Who runs it?

## 11. Section 7 — KPI, Budget, and the Clock

Primary KPI with baseline, target, and the date it is measured; 2 secondary KPIs; the daily pulse metrics (reach, sentiment, conversions vs. KPI, spend vs. discretionary). Close criteria: end date or KPI met, whichever first. Timeline strip: T0 workbook returned → brief approved → messages approved (clock starts) → T+72h assets live → daily pulse → close date → close-out report within 5 business days. Budget: ask, reserve before and after, what the money buys (paid media, creative, tooling), and the note that discretionary spend is reported in the QBR. Listening keywords to add, if any.

`ED INPUT`
a. What one number would make you call this a success? By when?
b. Budget cap for this campaign?
c. Hard stop date, even if the KPI is not met?

`CFT INPUT`
d. Is the primary KPI measurable with the UTMs and tools we have? If not, what is the nearest measurable proxy?

## 12. Section 8 — Risks and Guardrails

Table: risk, likelihood, impact, mitigation, owner. Standing rows: brand or trademark exposure; legal or antitrust (competitor comparisons, member names); community backlash or maintainer objection; competitor response; cannibalizing a planned campaign or fatiguing a shared list; shared-resource conflict; data or attribution gap; the campaign outliving its window. Guardrails restated: QA gate before publishing, one UTM per source, reversibility (what we can pull back and how fast), spend within reserve.

`ED INPUT`
a. Which of these risks would make you stop the campaign? What is the signal?

`CFT INPUT`
b. Who runs the QA gate, and what is on the checklist for this campaign?

## 13. Section 9 — Decision and Owner

Decision box: **Go / No-Go / Park (until [date or condition])**, decided by, on. Owner (who raised it), approver of messages, CFT leads by medium, the plan agent run owner. ED satisfaction check, to be answered now and again at close-out (this is the IDENTIFY-stage metric): (1) Was this worth flagging? (2) Was the workbook fast and complete enough to decide? (3) Would you run it as drafted? Each scored 1 to 5 with a one-line comment. Learnings we expect to carry into next quarter's plan workbook.

`ED INPUT`
a. Decision and date.
b. Satisfaction check, three scores and one line.

## 14. Next steps and handoff

Numbered: return the workbook by [date] → `hot-campaign-plan-agent` builds the Segmentation List and the Hot Campaign Plan from the approved workbook → messages approved, clock starts → content creation agents and source execution (QA gate, one UTM per source) → push to the Hot Campaigns dashboard → daily pulse → close-out report (attribution, outcomes, spend vs. discretionary, learnings to next quarter's plan). Name the agents where they exist (`linkedin-post-agent`, `case-study-agent`, `social-listening-report`, `run-marketing-impact-report`) and "manual: [owner/tool]" where they do not.

## Appendix

**A1. Parked opportunities** (scan mode, or any alternative found in flag mode): candidate, score, decay date, why parked, revisit date.

**A2. Data notes and provenance:** one row per source used: source, file or query, date, what it supplied, gaps. Includes the newsfeed folder ID and the listening workspace and keywords used.

**A3. Handoff block:** the structured summary defined in `handoff-contract.md`, in full, in both the slide body (compact) and the slide notes (complete).
